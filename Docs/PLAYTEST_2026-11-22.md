# Playtest notes, 2026-11-22 (round 88c)

What changed since the build you played on the 21st (round 88b), what to try, and what the measurements say.
Details and all tables: `Docs/STATUS.md`, "Round 88c". Stills: `Logs/r88c/`.

**Before you press Play: let the editor import first** (it recompiles and re-imports data when it gets focus after this
round was copied in). If the console shows "d3d11: failed to create 2D texture", close and reopen the editor once.

## What changed, per point of your notes

1. **Main menu: no ring round the boss.** A creature shown outside a fight wears no ground mark any more
   (`Logs/r88c/menu_no_ring.png`).
2. **"SURPRISE" is now "ALERT!"** on the ping label (`Logs/r88c/alert_label.png`).
3. **Is the game over when the boss dies? No, and it was checked in play:** on Landing Site the last wave's boss was
   killed with twelve creatures (its brood among them) still alive: the level went on, and it was won only when the
   last of them died. The rule in code is "every wave has started, the last one has sent its last creature, and nothing
   lives, whatever sent it". The counter behind it could in theory count one creature off twice; it now counts the
   living list itself. If you did see a level end with creatures on the field, tell us which level and wave: we could
   not make it happen.
4. **The boss's mark is quiet, and the boss is no longer buried.** The mark is one thin dull-red ring and two small
   chevrons (no white core, no glow band, no ripple). The real cause of the buried boss is fixed twice over:
   - The boss used to come out at the same moment as the plated walkers of its last cluster, and a Cragback (0.6)
     walks at a Brood Mother's pace (0.55): they covered it from the entrance to the base. **The boss now comes out
     eight seconds behind the last creature of its crowd** and closes the wave in plain view
     (`Logs/r88c/bosswave_2.png`: the Brood Mother alone on the road, its Cragbacks at the gate ahead).
   - **Its brood breaks out of its front and runs ahead** to a place of its own (1.2 to 3.3 cells in front), then
     walks on at its own pace. A brood creature too slow to get ahead of a fast boss comes out behind it instead.
5. **You can see the Brood Mother hit your towers** (`Logs/r88c/volley_sheet.png`): a flare of sparks at her head,
   three large bolts with a wake that fly to the three guns, a burst of the bolt's colour on each gun, the hit sound,
   and the gun rocks on its footing. The damage now lands when the bolt does (1.1 seconds after the launch), so the
   health bar drops at the moment of the hit. The numbers are the same (14 a bolt, three guns, every 3 seconds).
6. **Medium and Hard meet in the middle** (your note of the 21st: Hard about three times harder than Medium). Medium's
   creatures are about 12 % tougher, Hard's about 10 % softer and Hard's bosses 5 % smaller. Easy and Psycho were left
   where they were. On paper: STATUS, table "Medium against Hard" (Landing Site: Hard's crowd was x1.29 Medium's, now x1.03; Hard still has more bodies, a boss mid-level and harder bites).
7. **The first air raid of a level is announced and paid for.** Two waves before a level's first air raid the HUD says
   "ALERT! Air raid in 2 waves." (every difficulty). On Hard and Psycho the warning comes with coins: the price of the
   level's sky gun (120 for the Flak Turret), once per level (`Logs/r88c/air_raid_alert.png`; that still shows +50
   because the test save owned no sky gun: the purse is the price of the cheapest air gun you are offered). The late
   surprise itself is still there. And the toughest late wave is capped: a peak late in a level can no longer stack on
   top of the ramp (Shard Ridge wave 13 on Hard is 15 % softer than it would have been).

Also new: the next-wave preview says what a boss's brood is ("+brood" chip, or a plus on the number when the wave sends
that creature anyway); the five bosses whose brood call was silent have a sound; the Prowler scan text no longer says
the Cannon "hits the whole pack at once".
